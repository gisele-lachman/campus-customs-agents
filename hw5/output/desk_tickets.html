Reflection

My reflection on the real run (2026-10-14; shop date 2026-08-31). Every number below was checked against output/audit_trail.json, output/resolved_tickets.json, the ticket tabs and Cash tab, and the working database. Step counts come from the audit trail; durations come from the ticket_runs table.

1. How I'd evaluate the agents

I'd judge each ticket on the same six things: (a) did the facts come straight from the database, (b) were the shop rules followed (vendor blocked by an unpaid invoice, no negative cash, nothing sent to customers), (c) was human approval respected, (d) is the cash math right, (e) were the right agents used without wasted effort, and (f) how much time and how many steps it took.

2. Actual vs. Expected

  • 101 (Ticket 101 tab): the first call (Inventory) and the delegations I predicted (Inventory, Accounting, Customer Service) all happened, in that order. I'd correctly flagged that the 1-tee purchase order might be redundant, and it was prepared anyway. What I didn't predict was Accounting asking Inventory to double-check (the only delegation that went 2 levels deep). I also voided the customer draft (#3) instead of leaving it.
  • 102 (Ticket 102 tab): I'd predicted Facilities first, and listed the Boss calling Accounting directly as a plausible alternative. That's what happened, with Facilities and Accounting called in the same step, so there was no Facilities → Accounting handoff. The agents I expected to stay out stayed out.
  • 103 (Ticket 103 tab): the biggest difference. I expected a price override, a $264 purchase order for 12 hoodies from a blocked vendor, and a cash problem ($840 + $2,400 + $264 = $3,504 vs. $3,400). Instead, by the time 103 ran, I'd already paid invoice 501 on 101, so the vendor wasn't blocked anymore (list_vendors showed no open invoices). The Boss asked for a price instead of proposing a discount. No restock was prepared, so the shortfall never showed up. Even so, with only $160 left after the invoice and rent (Cash tab), the shop couldn't have paid for a $264 order. Approving the purchase order itself would have gone through, because approving a PO records it and moves no cash; the cash check only runs on payments. The trouble would have come when the vendor billed $264 against $160, and that payment would have been refused. The lesson: the tickets aren't independent, since what happens on one (paying 501) changes what's possible on the next.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

Ticket 102 is three lookups in a row: the lease, the cash balance, then prepare the payment (check_rent_status → get_cash_balance → prepare_payment, plus get_ticket, so 4 tool calls). The team used 10 tool calls, 2 delegations and 8 model replies. Ticket 101 is a straight chain: check stock, check the vendor, pay the invoice (check_stock → check_vendor_invoice → prepare_payment, plus the ticket, cash and draft). The team used 21 tool calls and 4 delegations. One agent with the same tools could have done both with far less delegation, in less time. The 103 stock mix-up came from a handoff: the question changed in transit (Accounting asked about "shortfall 12", and Inventory checked 12 instead of 20), and the Boss then had to reconcile two numbers. A single agent holding the ticket would have read the quantity once.

The team did do some things better. Only Accounting prepares payments, Customer Service only drafts, and the pay tool (execute_approved_payment) isn't even registered in the agents' MCP process; it's reachable only from the human approval route. Each agent has a narrow prompt, the audit trail shows who did every step (160 entries since the reset), and the Boss acts as a final check. For a shop this small I'd probably use one agent plus the same tools and keep the human approval gate, and use a team only where separate responsibility matters, like money and customer messages.

4. Three new problems this team could solve with the tools we built

  • A new unpaid vendor bill, like another Elm City Gifts invoice. The invoice and vendor tools (check_vendor_invoice, list_vendors) show what's owed and whether the vendor is blocked, Accounting prepares the payment (prepare_payment, kind invoice), and I approve it (tables: invoices, vendors, payments, cash_accounts). Caveat: no tool creates invoices or tickets, so the bill and a ticket for it have to be in the database first, and right now $160 would only cover a bill up to $160.
  • A customer asking for the Crest Mug, which has 0 in stock (CC-MUG-CREST, size OS). Inventory checks stock (check_stock), Elm City Gifts is the vendor (3-day lead time, earliest arrival 2026-09-03 from list_vendors), Accounting prepares a purchase order at the $3.50 unit cost (prepare_purchase_order; the list price is $14), and Customer Service drafts a reply with the expected date (prepare_customer_message) (tables: inventory, vendors, pricing). Caveat: it needs a ticket row first, because the prepare tools refuse an unknown ticket id.
  • A discount request that names a price, like 103 if the club had said what it would pay. Accounting checks the offer against the pricing table (hoodie cost $22, list $58) with get_pricing_margin and prepares it with prepare_price_override, which refuses anything below cost. Inventory checks stock, and Customer Service drafts the answer (tables: pricing, inventory).

5. Three new problems this team could not solve, and what I'd add

  • The issue with future cash. We ended with $160 (Cash tab), and the next $2,400 rent is due 2026-10-02, so the approve route would refuse that payment today. But we never modeled revenue (cash only goes out), so the team can't see money coming in. I'd need a sales and receipts table, a tool to record a sale or payment received, a cash forecast tool (upcoming rent, invoices, expected sales), and a Treasury agent that recommends which bills to pay first or delay.
  • Actually contacting people. The draft to Tauhid Zaman (101, #3, which I voided) and the question to the Yale AI Club (103, #5, still pending) never went anywhere. The tickets table only stores a requester's name, not an email, and there's no send tool at all by design. I'd need a customer contacts table, a send-email tool that needs my approval first, an inbox-reading tool so a reply can reopen a ticket like 103, and an Outreach agent to track replies.
  • Receiving stock. Approving the purchase order on 101 (#2) only records it as approved; nothing updates inventory when the tee arrives (around 2026-09-05), and there's no order status or delivery tracking. I'd need a purchase order status table, a receive-shipment tool that updates inventory (with a human check), and a Receiving agent that follows lead times and flags late deliveries.
